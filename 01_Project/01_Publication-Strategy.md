# Publication Strategy
## Auris Nexus Technologies (Pty) Ltd — Corporate Profile

**Document:** `01_Project/01_Publication-Strategy.md`
**Stage:** 1 — Project Planning
**Status:** Draft for approval

---

## 1. Strategic problem

South African ICT company profiles fail in one of three predictable ways.

**Failure mode 1 — The brochure.** Heavy on stock imagery and superlatives, thin on evidence. It
reads as a sales document, so procurement discounts it entirely and asks for the "real" documents.

**Failure mode 2 — The dossier.** Certificates, registration documents and organograms bolted
together with no narrative. It satisfies compliance but generates no preference; the buyer complies
with process and awards on price.

**Failure mode 3 — The borrowed voice.** Language lifted from global consultancies applied to a
business that does not operate at that scale. Sophisticated readers detect the mismatch immediately,
and it costs more credibility than plain speech would have.

The strategy for this publication is to occupy the space none of the three reach: **a narrative
document that is simultaneously a compliance instrument**, written in a register that matches the
company's actual scale and ambition rather than borrowing someone else's.

---

## 2. Positioning of the document

> **The Auris Nexus Corporate Profile is a due-diligence document that happens to be beautiful.**

Every design decision serves reader confidence, not decoration. Where a design choice and a
credibility choice conflict, credibility wins. Practically:

- Compliance and governance content receives the same design investment as the services sections.
  In most profiles it is relegated to grey appendices; here it is designed, and that signals a
  company that is unembarrassed by scrutiny.
- White space is used to signal confidence. Dense pages read as a company trying to seem larger
  than it is.
- The document never claims scale it does not have. It claims *precision*, which is defensible and
  is what mid-market ICT firms genuinely offer against the large integrators.

## 3. Competitive frame

The named benchmarks operate at different scales and their profiles do different work. We borrow
selectively rather than imitating wholesale.

| Benchmark | What is worth taking | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Deloitte** | Editorial discipline; typographic restraint; charts that carry argument | Institutional coldness; assumes reader already trusts the brand |
| **Accenture** | Bold colour used structurally; strong part dividers | Aggressive gradients; identity-heavy pages that say nothing |
| **IBM** | Grid rigour; the Plex-style engineered voice; diagram clarity | Density; the reader must already be technical |
| **Microsoft** | Accessibility discipline; plain-language capability statements | Product-catalogue feel |
| **AWS** | Technical credibility through architecture diagrams | Diagram overload; near-zero narrative |
| **Oracle** | Comparison tables that survive scrutiny | Heavy-handed competitive framing |
| **SA ICT leaders** (BCX, Datatec, EOH, Adapt IT) | Local compliance fluency — B-BBEE, POPIA, PFMA, SITA context handled natively | Template sameness; a reader can't tell them apart |

**The differentiating move:** the benchmarks are all *large-scale* profiles that rely on the reader
already knowing the brand. Auris Nexus cannot borrow that assumption. Our profile must therefore do
something theirs need not — **earn trust from a cold start, page by page**. That is the design brief.

## 4. Three-layer reading structure

The single most important structural decision. Every spread is built so three different readers
extract value at three different depths.

| Layer | Reading time | Carried by | Reader served |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer 1 — Scan** | 30 seconds per spread | Section titles, standfirsts, pull-facts, iconography, table headers | Procurement officer, executive |
| **Layer 2 — Skim** | 3 minutes per spread | Subheadings, bulleted outcomes, callout boxes, diagram captions, table rows | Line manager, evaluator |
| **Layer 3 — Study** | 10 minutes per spread | Body copy, methodology detail, technical specification, footnotes | Technical evaluator, risk officer |

**Rule:** a reader who reads *only* Layer 1 across the whole document must still finish with an
accurate and complete understanding of what the company does and why it is safe to appoint. Layer 1
is not a teaser for Layer 3 — it is a self-sufficient document embedded inside a larger one.

This is what makes a single publication serve both a five-minute municipal reviewer and a
thirty-minute bank risk assessor, and it is why the derivative editions in the Charter §5 can be
*cut* rather than rewritten: the Short-form is Layers 1–2, the Executive Brief is Layer 1.

## 5. Voice and register

### The voice in one line
**Measured, specific, and unhurried — the register of a senior practitioner explaining their work to
a peer who is intelligent but not a specialist.**

### Characteristics

| Attribute | Applied as |
|---|---|
| **Declarative** | Statements, not pitches. "We deliver X" — not "We are passionate about delivering X" |
| **Concrete** | Named technologies, named standards, named regulations, quantified outcomes |
| **Restrained** | Confidence signalled through precision and calm, never through volume |
| **Plain** | Technical terms used when they are the correct term, explained when the reader may not share them |
| **Accountable** | The document acknowledges limits. A profile that admits nothing is believed on nothing |

### Person and tense
- **First person plural** ("we", "our") for the company's own actions and commitments.
- **Third person** ("Auris Nexus Technologies") for formal and legal statements, and on first mention
  in any section that may be extracted and read standalone.
- **Present tense** for capability. **Future tense only under explicitly labelled roadmap headings.**

### Proscribed language

Never used in this document:

> cutting-edge · world-class · best-of-breed · synergy · leverage (as a verb) · seamless(ly) ·
> robust solutions · one-stop shop · turnkey · game-changing · revolutionary · passionate about ·
> we pride ourselves on · in today's fast-paced world · unlock/unleash potential · holistic ·
> paradigm shift · state-of-the-art · dynamic team · thinking outside the box · bespoke (overused —
> prefer "purpose-built") · digital transformation journey · empower (unless describing an actual
> permission model)

**Why this list exists.** These phrases are load-bearing in weak profiles and absent from strong
ones. Each is a placeholder where a specific claim should be. When a draft reaches for one, the
correct response is not to substitute a synonym but to supply the missing specific.

### Sentence craft
- Vary sentence length deliberately. A short sentence after two long ones carries weight.
- Lead paragraphs with the conclusion. Institutional readers do not read to the end of paragraphs.
- Maximum three sentences per body paragraph in scanning contexts; five in Layer 3 narrative.
- Active voice by default. Passive is permitted where the actor is genuinely irrelevant
  ("the system is audited quarterly") — not to obscure accountability.

### Numerals and units
- Numbers under ten in words, ten and above in figures — except in tables, statistics panels and
  technical specifications, where figures are always used.
- South African English spelling throughout: *organisation, recognise, programme* (of work),
  *centre, licence* (noun) / *license* (verb), *analyse, catalogue, defence*.
- Currency as **R1 250 000** or **R1,25 million** — space as thousands separator, comma as decimal,
  per SANS 920 / SI convention.
- Dates as **2 August 2026**. Never 02/08/2026 — ambiguous to international readers.

## 6. Narrative architecture

The document follows a deliberate persuasive sequence. Each part answers the question raised by the
part before it.

```
FRONT MATTER    "Who is speaking, and why should I keep reading?"
      ↓
PART 1  COMPANY     → establishes identity and intent      "Who are you?"
      ↓                                                     raises: "But what do you do?"
PART 2  SERVICES    → establishes capability                "What can you do?"
      ↓                                                     raises: "Do you understand MY world?"
PART 3  INDUSTRIES  → establishes contextual fluency        "Do you know my sector?"
      ↓                                                     raises: "Can you actually build it?"
PART 4  TECHNICAL   → establishes engineering depth         "Can you build it?"
      ↓                                                     raises: "Can you deliver it reliably?"
PART 5  DELIVERY    → establishes execution discipline      "Will it land?"
      ↓                                                     raises: "Are you a safe supplier?"
PART 6  EXCELLENCE  → establishes risk posture              "Are you safe?"
      ↓                                                     raises: "Prove it."
PART 7  CREDENTIALS → supplies evidence                     "Prove it."
      ↓
CLOSING         "How do I engage you?"
```

The sequence is load-bearing. Credentials appear **last** because evidence is persuasive only once
the reader knows what it is evidence *of*; a registration certificate on page 6 is administrative
noise, the same certificate on page 87 is confirmation. Conversely, the **Company at a Glance**
panel sits in the front matter so that a checklist reader who needs facts immediately is never
forced to hunt.

## 7. Editorial policy on evidence

Because this document enters regulated procurement processes, evidence handling is a stated policy,
not a matter of taste.

| Claim type | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Legal / registration status | Verbatim from official documents; certificate references cited |
| Compliance status (tax, B-BBEE) | Current certificate details, with validity dates stated |
| Certifications held | Named with certifying body and scope; only if held on date of publication |
| Certifications planned | Under "Certification Roadmap" only, with target dates, clearly future-tense |
| Client names | Only with documented written permission; otherwise anonymised by sector and scale |
| Case study metrics | Only where measured; method stated where a number could be challenged |
| Testimonials | Attributed with name, role and organisation, or omitted. Never anonymous-but-implied |
| Team credentials | Qualifications as awarded; institutions named; no inflation of titles |
| Partnerships | Formal partner status only, at the tier actually held |

Where a fact is unavailable, the section is written to be complete and credible *without* it, rather
than left visibly hollow. This is a writing discipline, and it is the difference between a young
company that reads as substantial and one that reads as thin.

## 8. Design language in one paragraph

**Engineered clarity.** The visual system takes its cue from technical documentation and
infrastructure schematics rather than from advertising: a strict grid, generous white space, a deep
navy authority colour, a single high-signal cyan used as a wayfinding accent, and rules and keylines
that behave like a diagram legend. Photography is documentary rather than aspirational. Diagrams are
drawn as though they were engineering drawings, because in this business they effectively are. The
overall impression is intended to be *precise, calm and expensive* — a document that looks like it
was made by people who are careful, which is the single quality every one of our nine audiences is
actually shopping for.

## 9. Success criteria for the finished publication

The document succeeds if:

1. A procurement officer can locate every mandatory returnable fact within 60 seconds.
2. A bank's risk assessor finds the security and continuity content sufficient to proceed without a
   supplementary questionnaire.
3. Any single spread can be extracted, printed and handed over as a standalone leave-behind without
   looking amputated.
4. A reader who knows nothing of the company finishes Part 1 able to describe it accurately.
5. Nothing in the document requires correction under scrutiny.
6. It does not look like any other South African ICT company profile.
