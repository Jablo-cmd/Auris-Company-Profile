# Publication Strategy
## Auris Nexus Technologies (Pty) Ltd — Corporate Profile

## 1. Strategic purpose

The profile must do more than introduce Auris Nexus. It must help a reader move from:

**“Who are they?” → “Can they solve this?” → “Can I trust the way they work?” → “What should we discuss next?”**

That means the document balances commercial clarity, technical credibility and institutional readiness.

## 2. Positioning

Auris Nexus should be presented as a **South African technology partner that designs and builds practical digital systems around how organisations actually work.**

The strongest positioning is not company size. It is the ability to connect:

**business understanding + software engineering + automation + digital experience + ongoing improvement.**

## 3. Commercial voice

The profile should sound:

- confident
- clear
- capable
- commercially aware
- technically credible
- honest about evidence
- easy for a non-technical decision-maker to understand

Avoid both extremes:

**Too weak:** “We are a small company offering various IT services.”

**Too inflated:** “A world-class, leading, end-to-end digital transformation powerhouse.”

The preferred register is:

**“We understand the business problem, design the right solution, build it properly and stay involved as it evolves.”**

## 4. Conversion architecture

Every major section should answer at least one of these questions:

| Reader question | Profile response |
|---|---|
| Who are you? | Company identity, ownership, positioning and purpose |
| What do you do? | Solution portfolio |
| Is it relevant to me? | Business contexts and sector coverage |
| Can you actually build it? | Engineering capability |
| Have you done meaningful work? | Selected products, platforms and case studies |
| How will you work with us? | Delivery approach and engagement models |
| What about risk? | Security, privacy, governance and credentials |
| What next? | Clear contact and engagement path |

## 5. Evidence hierarchy

Use evidence in this order:

1. A delivered Auris product, platform or engagement
2. A demonstrable technical artefact or repository-backed capability
3. A documented company credential
4. A clear current capability statement
5. A labelled planned capability

Never use an adjective where evidence can do the work.

## 6. Reader experience

The profile should work at three levels:

### Scan
Headlines, facts, solution names, project names and diagrams tell the story.

### Skim
Short paragraphs, bullets, case-study summaries and capability tables provide substance.

### Study
Technical, governance and delivery detail provides confidence for evaluators who need more.

The scan layer must remain accurate on its own.

## 7. Editorial rules

- Use South African English.
- Prefer short, direct sentences.
- Use technical terminology when it adds precision.
- Explain specialist terms when the audience may not know them.
- Lead with business value and practical outcomes.
- Separate capability from proven outcome.
- Label planned capability.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives.
- Avoid filler pages.
- Do not repeat the same company description throughout the document.

### Avoid

“world-class”, “leading”, “cutting-edge”, “best-in-class”, “seamless”, “turnkey”, “revolutionary”, “game-changing”, “one-stop shop”, “robust solutions”, “passionate about”, “state-of-the-art”.

The solution is not to replace these with different adjectives. The solution is to state something specific.

## 8. Visual strategy

The visual system should communicate **engineering precision with human accessibility**.

Use:

- Nexus Navy as the principal structural colour
- Auris Cyan / Signal Cyan as controlled accents
- strong black/white text contrast
- diagrams for systems and processes
- tables for factual comparison
- project imagery where it proves real work
- restrained iconography
- consistent page rhythm

Avoid:

- decorative technology imagery with no connection to the copy
- excessive gradients
- generic server-room stock photography
- visual clutter
- oversized claims presented as statistics

## 9. Commercial conversion points

The profile should contain clear opportunities to continue the conversation:

- “Discuss a business problem”
- “Request a capability discussion”
- “Explore a pilot”
- “Discuss a technology partnership”
- “Request a proposal”

The document should make the next step feel straightforward rather than pressured.

## 10. Master narrative

The complete profile follows:

**Auris Nexus → What we solve → What we build → Evidence → How we engineer → How we deliver → How we govern → How to engage**

That sequence is now the controlling narrative for all future editions.
