## 1. Structural model

The publication is organised in four descending levels of granularity. This hierarchy is fixed and
governs numbering, navigation, styles and cross-references throughout the project.

```
PART            7 parts + front matter + closing
  └── SECTION   numbered X.Y, one or two pages, self-contained
        └── SUBSECTION   numbered X.Y.Z, never appears in the contents
              └── COMPONENT   callout, table, diagram, card, fact panel
```

**The section is the atomic unit of the document.** Every section is designed to be
extractable — it opens with its own standfirst, never depends on a facing page for meaning, and
carries enough context to be read cold. This is what makes the derivative editions (Charter §5)
producible by extraction rather than rewriting, and it is why a sales consultant can pull a single
spread for a client meeting without it reading as a fragment.

Deeper than three levels is prohibited. If content appears to require a fourth level, the section is
mis-structured and must be split.

### Pagination rules governing this structure

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| Total extent divisible by 4 | Print signature requirement; avoids blank padding at bind |
| Every part extent is an **even** number | Keeps all subsequent part dividers on the same side |
| Every part divider falls on a **recto** (odd) page | Standard corporate-report convention; the divider is seen on page-turn, not alongside preceding content |
| Two-page sections set on **even–odd** pairs wherever flow permits | A true spread; the reader sees both halves simultaneously |
| Single-page sections never split across a turn | Each is a complete visual statement |

Extent is fixed at **112 pages**, the smallest multiple of four that accommodates the mandated
content at the specified density without crowding. Final pagination is locked at Stage 11 once
real copy lengths are known; the structure above is the design target and the tolerance is ±4 pages.

## 5. Navigation system

Institutional readers navigate by hunting, not by reading sequentially. Five devices support this.

| Device | Placement | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Part colour coding** | Divider pages, running heads, contents | Each part carries a fixed tint from the accent ramp; a reader flicking the block edge finds a part without reading |
| **Thumb index** | Outer edge of each divider page | Seven vertical positions, one per part; makes the printed block edge navigable |
| **Running heads** | Verso: part name · Recto: section title | Verso and recto differ, so an open spread self-locates |
| **Part contents** | On each divider page | A local table of contents for that part, with page numbers |
| **Cross-references** | Inline, live Word fields | `see 4.4 Cybersecurity, p. 65` — auto-updating, never typed page numbers |

## 6. Content reuse map

Several facts must appear in more than one place for different readers. Uncontrolled, this is what
makes profiles contradict themselves. Each repeated fact has one **owner section**; every other
appearance is a cross-reference or a Word bookmark reference to the owner.

| Fact | Owner section | Also appears in |
|---|---|---|
| Registration number | 7.2 | Company at a glance (p.8); back cover |
| B-BBEE level | 7.4 | Company at a glance; 7.1 capability statement |
| Tax compliance status | 7.3 | Company at a glance; 7.1 |
| Service list | 2.0 | 1.1 executive summary; 7.1 |
| Industries served | 3.0 | 7.1 |
| Technology stack | 4.0 | Service sections 2.1–2.8 (per-service subset) |
| Delivery lifecycle | 5.0 | 1.6 business philosophy (summary only) |
| Contact details | 8.2 | Back cover; inside front cover |
| Company statistics | 7.7 | 1.1; company at a glance |

**Rule:** if a fact changes, it changes in the owner section only. Stage 11 assembly verifies every
dependent reference resolves and every bookmark updates.
