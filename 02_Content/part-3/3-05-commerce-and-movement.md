# 3.5 Commerce and movement

**Layout:** Archetype D, narrative spread. Three industries, each with the fixed Challenges ·
Solutions · Business value · Technology opportunities structure.

---

## Copy

**Standfirst**

> Retail, logistics and transport are connected by a single operational fact: a customer's experience
> is determined by what happens after the order, not at it — and that is where most systems in this
> cluster are weakest.

---

### Regulatory context

This cluster carries lighter sector-specific regulation than public sector or financial services, but
consumer protection and data-privacy obligations apply fully to every customer-facing system, and
transport operators carry additional compliance obligations tied to vehicle, driver and cargo
regulation. The operational challenge here is less about regulatory constraint and more about
systems integration across genuinely complex physical and information supply chains.

---

### Retail

**Challenges.** Inventory, point-of-sale and e-commerce systems that frequently do not share a single
view of stock, producing overselling or stranded inventory. Customer experience expectations shaped
by omnichannel leaders far larger than most retailers can individually match. Promotional and pricing
logic scattered across systems rather than centrally managed.

**Solutions.** Inventory and channel integration that gives a single, accurate stock view across
physical and digital channels. E-commerce and point-of-sale systems built or integrated to reduce, not
multiply, reconciliation work.

**Business value.** Reduced overselling and stranded-stock incidents. Improved customer experience
consistency across channels. Less manual reconciliation between sales and inventory systems.

**Technology opportunities.** Omnichannel inventory and order management. E-commerce platform
development and integration. Customer data and loyalty system integration.

---

### Logistics and transport

**Challenges.** Fleet and cargo visibility that lags the pace of customer expectation for real-time
tracking. Route and resource planning still done manually in many operations, at real cost in fuel and
time. Compliance and reporting obligations tied to vehicle, driver and cargo regulation, often
tracked separately from operational systems.

**Solutions.** Fleet telematics and tracking integration that feeds both operational planning and
customer-facing visibility from one data source. Route optimisation and resource planning tooling
built to the operation's actual constraints, not a generic logistics template.

**Business value.** Reduced fuel and resource cost from improved route planning. Improved customer
satisfaction from accurate, real-time visibility. Simplified compliance reporting drawn directly from
operational systems.

**Technology opportunities.** Fleet telematics and tracking systems. Route and resource optimisation
tooling. Warehouse and cargo management systems.
