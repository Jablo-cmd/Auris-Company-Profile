# 3.1 Public sector

**Layout:** Archetype D, narrative spread. Three industries treated in sequence, each with the fixed
Challenges · Solutions · Business value · Technology opportunities structure.

## Copy

**Standfirst**

> Government, municipalities and state-owned enterprises operate under a procurement and governance
> regime unlike the private sector's — PFMA, MFMA, SITA and Treasury regulation shape not just what
> is bought, but how.

---

### Regulatory context

Public sector technology procurement sits inside the Public Finance Management Act, the Municipal
Finance Management Act, and — for organs of state within its mandate — SITA's procurement framework,
with Treasury regulations governing supply chain process throughout. A system built without reference
to that framework, however well engineered, creates compliance exposure for the client that no amount
of technical quality offsets. We design and procure within that reality rather than around it.

---

### Government (national and provincial departments)

**Challenges.** Legacy systems that predate current compliance requirements. Procurement cycles that
outlast the technology being procured. Service delivery measured by the public against private-sector
digital expectations, on public-sector budgets and timelines.

**Solutions.** Modernisation approaches that respect data continuity and audit requirements. Systems
specified and procured to survive a PFMA-compliant procurement cycle without losing technical
currency before go-live.

**Business value.** Reduced service-delivery backlog. Auditable systems that withstand Auditor-
General scrutiny. Lower total cost of ownership than repeated tactical patching of legacy systems.

**Technology opportunities.** Citizen-facing digital services. Case and workflow management.
Data consolidation across departmental silos, within POPIA and governance constraints.

---

### Municipalities

**Challenges.** Billing and revenue systems under sustained public and political scrutiny. Constrained
technical capacity relative to the scale of systems municipalities depend on. Infrastructure spanning
widely varying connectivity conditions across a municipal area.

**Solutions.** Billing and revenue management systems built for accuracy and auditability, since a
municipal billing error is a resident's lived experience, not an abstraction. Systems designed for the
municipality's actual technical capacity to operate them, not an idealised capacity.

**Business value.** Improved revenue collection accuracy. Reduced billing disputes and their
administrative cost. Systems a constrained technical team can genuinely sustain after our involvement
ends.

**Technology opportunities.** Revenue and billing platforms. Asset and infrastructure management.
Citizen query and case management systems.

---

### State-owned enterprises

**Challenges.** Governance and reporting obligations that combine public-sector accountability with
commercial operating pressure. Legacy core systems carrying decades of institutional dependency.
Cybersecurity exposure proportionate to national-infrastructure significance in some SOEs.

**Solutions.** Modernisation sequenced to avoid disrupting operations the country depends on.
Governance and reporting systems built to satisfy both shareholder-style oversight and public
accountability simultaneously.

**Business value.** Reduced operational risk from legacy dependency. Reporting that satisfies
multiple, sometimes competing, oversight bodies from one system of record.

**Technology opportunities.** Enterprise resource planning modernisation. Operational technology and
information technology integration where relevant. Governance and compliance reporting platforms.
