# 3.7 Sector engagement and procurement routes

**Layout:** Archetype G, full-width table page.

---

## Copy

**Standfirst**

> How a sector is engaged is often a compliance question before it is a commercial one. This table
> maps the routes actually available per sector, so a procurement reader can find their own route
> without hunting.

Procurement mechanism is frequently the first practical filter on whether a supplier can even be
considered, independent of capability. The table below states which routes apply where, in the sector
clusters set out on the preceding pages, so a reader from any sector can locate their applicable route
directly rather than inferring it from general commercial language.

---

## Table 3.1 — Procurement routes by sector cluster

| Sector cluster | Open tender | RFQ | Panel / transversal contract | Direct appointment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Public sector | ✅ Primary route | ✅ Below threshold | ✅ Where listed | Regulation-permitting only |
| Knowledge and human services | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Where listed | ✅ Private institutions |
| Financial services | ✅ | ✅ | — | ✅ |
| Industrial and resources | ✅ | ✅ | — | ✅ |
| Commerce and movement | ✅ | ✅ | — | ✅ |
| Property, security and professional services, SMEs | ✅ | ✅ | — | ✅ |

---

## Production notes

- Public-sector routes are constrained by PFMA, MFMA, SITA and Treasury regulation, as set out in
  §3.1 — "direct appointment" there is materially narrower than in private-sector clusters and must
  not be read as generally available.
- Table built as a native Word table, `AN Table` style. ✅ marks availability; a dash marks a route
  not typically applicable to that cluster, not a route we decline.
- This page closes Part Three. No closing narrative flourish — the table is the destination content,
  consistent with the "structure, not sentiment" convention used to close Part One at 1.10.
