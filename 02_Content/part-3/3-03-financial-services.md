# 3.3 Financial services

**Layout:** Archetype D, narrative spread. Two industries, each with the fixed Challenges · Solutions
· Business value · Technology opportunities structure.

## Copy

**Standfirst**

---

### Regulatory context

The South African Reserve Bank and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority regulate different aspects
of the same institutions — prudential soundness and market conduct respectively — and the Financial
Advisory and Intermediary Services Act governs how financial products are represented to customers
through any system, including software interfaces. Core system change in this sector carries
governance overhead most other industries do not encounter, and we scope for that overhead
deliberately rather than discovering it mid-engagement.

---

### Banking

**Challenges.** Core banking systems old enough that change is expensive and risky, but replacement is
riskier still if poorly sequenced. Fraud and financial crime patterns evolving faster than annual
system review cycles typically accommodate. Customer expectation of digital-channel parity with
significantly larger institutions.

**Solutions.** Modernisation sequenced around core-system risk tolerance — incremental where the core
demands it, more direct where a peripheral system permits. Fraud analytics and monitoring capability
scoped as a continuous programme, not a single delivered project.

**Business value.** Reduced operational risk from core-system fragility. Improved fraud detection
response time. Digital-channel capability that competes on the features customers actually use, not
on feature-count alone.

**Technology opportunities.** Core system modernisation and peripheral service layers. Fraud analytics
and monitoring. Digital and self-service banking channels.

---

### Insurance

**Challenges.** Policy administration and claims systems frequently fragmented across product lines,
built up over years rather than designed as one system. Claims processing speed under increasing
customer and regulatory expectation. FAIS conduct obligations applying to every customer-facing
digital touchpoint, not only human-mediated advice.

**Solutions.** Policy and claims administration integration across product lines, so a customer's full
relationship with the insurer is visible from one system rather than reconstructed manually.
Conduct-aware design in customer-facing digital interfaces, built with FAIS obligations as an
architectural input.

**Business value.** Faster claims turnaround. Reduced compliance exposure from inconsistent
conduct-obligation handling across digital touchpoints. Consolidated customer view supporting better
retention and cross-sell decisions made on complete information.

**Technology opportunities.** Policy administration and claims platforms. Customer data integration
across product lines. Conduct-compliant digital advice and self-service tooling.
