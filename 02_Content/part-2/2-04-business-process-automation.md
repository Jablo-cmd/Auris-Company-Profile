# 2.4 Business Process Automation

**Layout:** Archetype H, the fixed ten-block service template.

## ① Service title

Business Process Automation
*Service covered: business process automation*

---

## ② Standfirst (40–55 words)

> Automating a broken process makes it fail faster and harder to trace. We map the process first,
> fix what should not be automated as-is, and only then remove the manual steps that remain.

---

## ③ Description

Business Process Automation replaces manual, repetitive, rule-based work with software that performs
it consistently — data entry between systems that do not talk to each other, approval routing,
scheduled reporting, document processing, and the reconciliation work that currently depends on a
person doing the same steps correctly every time. The value is not the automation itself; it is the
elimination of the variance and delay that manual repetition introduces.

The discipline that matters most in this service is sequencing: automating a process before
understanding why it exists in its current form encodes its inefficiencies permanently and makes them
harder to change, because they are now embedded in software rather than in a habit someone could
question. We map the process, identify what should change before automation and what genuinely should
not, and automate what is left.

---

## ④ Problems this solves

- Staff time is consumed by repetitive, rule-based tasks that do not require judgement
- Manual handoffs between systems introduce delay and transcription error
- Approval and reporting processes are inconsistent because they depend on individual discipline
  rather than a defined workflow
- A process is too inefficient to automate as-is, but nobody has had the mandate to redesign it first

---

## ⑤ Business benefits

- Consistent execution of rule-based work, independent of individual workload or attention
- Faster cycle time on processes previously gated by manual handoff
- A documented, auditable record of what happened and when, rather than institutional memory
- Staff time redirected from repetitive execution to the judgement-based work automation cannot do

---

## ⑥ Typical deliverables

- Current-state process map and inefficiency assessment
- Target-state process design, agreed before automation begins
- Automated workflow, built and tested against the target-state design
- Exception-handling design for cases the automation should not resolve alone
- Audit trail and reporting on automated process performance
- User and process-owner training
- Post-deployment tuning period
- Handover documentation for ongoing process ownership

## ⑧ Ideal client profile

An organisation with a defined, rule-based process currently executed manually at meaningful volume
or frequency, with the internal mandate to redesign the process rather than automate it unchanged.

---

## ⑨ Expected outcomes

- Reduced cycle time on the automated process
- Fewer transcription and handoff errors between systems
- An auditable record of process execution, available on demand rather than reconstructed
- Staff time reallocated away from repetitive execution toward judgement-based work

---

## ⑩ Industries served

Particularly relevant in financial services, government, manufacturing, logistics and transport, and
professional services, where high-volume rule-based processes are most common — though applicable
wherever a qualifying process exists.
