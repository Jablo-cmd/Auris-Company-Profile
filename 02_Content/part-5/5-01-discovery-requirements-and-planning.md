# 5.1 Discovery, requirements and planning

**Standfirst**

> Phases 1–3 of the lifecycle in 5.0. This is the unbillable-time stage described in 1.6 — where the
> operating problem is tested before a solution is proposed for it.

---

## Copy

**Phase 1 — Discovery and stakeholder engagement.** We engage the people who will use the system, not
only the people who commissioned it, per the proximity principle described in 1.5. The gate for this
phase is a documented understanding of the current-state process, agreed with the client before
requirements are drafted against it.

**Phase 2 — Requirements definition.** Requirements are traced back to the discovery findings that
justify them, so that a requirement can always be defended against the problem it addresses rather
than existing because it was requested. The gate is a requirements baseline the client has reviewed
and accepted.

**Phase 3 — Planning and scoping.** Scope, sequencing and commercial model (per 2.9) are agreed against
the requirements baseline. Where the requirement is precisely understood, this supports a fixed-price
plan; where it will genuinely evolve, it supports a time-and-materials or phased approach instead, per
the reasoning in 2.9. The gate is a signed-off plan and, for fixed-scope engagements, a fixed-price
agreement.

**Inputs, outputs and gate criteria** for each phase are formalised as **[[DISCOVERY_ARTEFACTS]]** —
the specific document templates and sign-off mechanism used, confirmed rather than described
generically here.
