# 5.0 The delivery lifecycle — master diagram

**Layout:** Archetype F, extended across the spread. Master diagram dominant: fifteen phases across
five stages, with gates, artefacts and roles marked. Every later section in Part Five references this
diagram rather than restating its structure.

## Copy

**Standfirst**

---

### Copy

The lifecycle below describes **what happens**, in what order, and what has to be true before work
moves from one phase to the next. It is deliberately separate from the question of **which named
methodology** — agile, waterfall, or a hybrid suited to the engagement — governs how a given phase is
actually run. That distinction matters: the shape of delivery is consistent across our engagements;
the specific method applied within it is chosen to fit the client's environment and is stated per
engagement, not asserted here as a single house methodology.

**Discover** (phases 1–3) establishes what the client actually needs, tested against how the
organisation actually works, per the discipline described in 1.6. **Design** (phases 4–6) turns that
understanding into an architecture and an experience. **Build** (phases 7–10) is where the system is
constructed and proven, including the security gate described in 5.4 — no phase in this stage is
considered complete without its exit criteria met. **Deploy** (phases 11–12) moves the system into
production and the people who will use it into readiness. **Sustain** (phases 13–15) covers support,
maintenance and the review cycle that determines what the next iteration should be.

**Delivery methodology applied in practice:** [[DELIVERY_METHODOLOGY]] — stated per engagement once
confirmed, not defaulted to a single named framework.
