# 1.4 Vision and mission

> ⚠ **The vision and mission below are PROPOSED, not recorded.** No existing statements were
> supplied. These are drafted for the company's approval or amendment. They are the only content in
> Part One that asserts something the company has not yet stated about itself, and they are marked as
> such until confirmed. See `01_Project/05_Fact-Register.md` item **B3**.

**Standfirst**

> A vision describes the position we intend to hold. A mission describes what we do every day to
> reach it. The distinction matters, and most organisations blur it.

---

## Copy

Vision and mission statements have a poor reputation, largely deserved. They are frequently
interchangeable, usually unfalsifiable, and rarely capable of settling an actual decision. We have
written ours to be tested: each one should be able to tell us, in a real situation, what to do.

### Our vision

> **To be the technology partner South African organisations trust with the systems their work
> depends on.**

Three words in that statement are load-bearing. **Trust** is a measurable outcome, not a sentiment —
it appears as repeat engagement, as reference-ability, and as clients bringing us problems before
they have written a specification. **Depends on** narrows our ambition deliberately: we are not
pursuing discretionary or experimental technology work, but the operational systems an organisation
cannot function without. And **South African** is a commitment to a market and its regulatory
reality, not a temporary base of operations.

### Our mission

> **To design, build and operate software that makes organisations measurably better at what they
> already do — combining engineering discipline with a working understanding of the environments we
> serve.**

**Measurably** is the word we expect to be held to. If an engagement cannot state what will improve
and how it will be observed, we have not understood the problem well enough to begin. **Already do**
is a statement of restraint: most organisations do not need to be reinvented, they need the friction
removed from work they are performing adequately under difficulty. And **working understanding**
means sector fluency earned through delivery, not a claim of expertise assembled from research.

### The distinction, in practice

The vision is a destination and is not achieved in any single year. The mission is what we do this
week, and it is the statement we use to settle arguments. When a proposed feature cannot be tied to a
measurable improvement in work the client already performs, the mission says decline it — and that is
the test of whether a mission statement is doing any work at all.
