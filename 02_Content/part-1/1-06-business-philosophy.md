# 1.6 Business philosophy

**Standfirst**

> How we think about client work — and the position we take on the difference between supplying
> technology and being responsible for what it does.

---

## Copy

**We treat the operating problem as the brief, not the software request.**

Clients approach us with a solution already named: a portal, an app, an automation, a migration. That
name is useful information but it is not the brief. It is a hypothesis someone formed about a problem
they were experiencing, and our first obligation is to test it. Occasionally the hypothesis is right
and we build what was asked for. Frequently it is directionally right but wrongly scoped. Sometimes
the correct answer is a change to a process and no software at all, and we are willing to say so.

This is unbillable time at the front of an engagement, and we accept the cost because the alternative
is worse: a system that satisfies its specification and fails its purpose, which is the single most
common outcome in this industry.

**We build for the day after handover, not the day of it.**

Most software is designed for the delivery event — the demonstration, the sign-off, the invoice.
Almost none of a system's life is spent in that moment. Its real character emerges over years, under
staff turnover, changing regulation, growing data volumes and integrations nobody anticipated. We
design for that period: conservative about complexity, explicit about failure modes, and deliberate
about documentation, because the person who will need it has not been hired yet.

**We prefer subtraction.**

Every feature is paid for annually — in maintenance, in training, in the cognitive cost to users, and
in the surface area available to fail. The discipline is not adding capability; it is declining to.
Our proposals routinely include what we recommend against, and that section is often the most useful
part of the document.

**We are a partner where that is warranted, and a supplier where it is not.**

"Partnership" is used loosely in this industry, usually to justify a longer contract. We use it
precisely. A partnership means we are accountable for an outcome, we have visibility of the business
context that produces it, and we are prepared to argue with you about it. Where a client wants a
defined piece of work delivered competently to a specification, that is a supply relationship, it is
entirely legitimate, and we do not inflate it into something else in order to charge more.

**We would rather lose the work than misrepresent the fit.**

An engagement we are not equipped to deliver costs more to exit than it ever earned. Declining is the
cheaper failure, for both parties, and we take it early.
