# 6.2 Risk management and business continuity

**Standfirst**

> A newly established, single-person company faces a specific continuity question every evaluator will
> already be thinking about. It is addressed directly here rather than left for the reader to raise.

---

## Copy

Risk management at a one-person company concentrates around a single, obvious question: what happens
to a client's engagement if the founder is unavailable. This profile does not overstate the answer.
Auris Nexus does not currently operate a formal, documented business continuity or disaster recovery
plan covering founder unavailability, and this is stated plainly rather than implied away with generic
risk-management language.

What is already in place, and genuinely evidenced rather than asserted: source code and documentation
are held in version control for every Auris-developed system (4.1, 4.5), and client deliverables are
scoped with source access and documentation as contractual deliverables (1.8 §5) — both of which reduce
the practical impact of founder unavailability on a client's ability to continue with the work product
itself, independent of whether Auris continues to support it.

As Auris Nexus grows beyond its current single-founder structure, formalising a documented business
continuity plan — succession arrangements, a defined recovery process for delivery interruption, and a
named continuity contact beyond the founder — is the intended next step. It is stated here as intent,
not as a current capability.
